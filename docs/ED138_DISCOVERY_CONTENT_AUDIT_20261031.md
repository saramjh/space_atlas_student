## Scientific source hierarchy

1. Rubin Observatory official discovery announcement (2026-10-27): https://rubinobservatory.org/news/rubin-first-dwarf-planet-candidate ; finds object in 2026 survey data, ~46 AU present distance at announcement, provisional designation 2026 ED138, *candidate* status.
2. Holman et al. discovery preprint (2026-10-28), arXiv:2610.38693: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38693 ; H_V=3.19, diameter 612–1,530 km under geometric albedo 0.25–0.04, a=43.09 AU, e=.139, i=24.4°, near Neptune 12:7 resonance, orbit classification/membership unsettled. Its estimated diameter is not directly measured and its surface not resolved.
3. IAU official naming/classification https://iauarchive.eso.org/public/themes/naming/ ; **five** recognized dwarf planets (Ceres, Pluto, Haumea, Makemake, Eris). Important distinction: candidate discovery by IAU Minor Planet Center is not IAU dwarf-planet recognition.
4. NASA Pluto diameter https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/pluto/facts/ ; approximately 2,377 km, used only as a common linear *diameter* comparison.
5. MIT TESS precovery announcement (2026-10-30) https://tess.mit.edu/news/seven-years-of-tess-precovery-observations-of-the-dwarf-planet-candidate-2026-ed/ ; stacked prior detections support the orbit, not resolved surface or equilibrium shape.

## Learner question and experiment

Question: how can the same observed brightness imply a large spread of inferred diameters? Hold measured H_V=3.19 fixed, vary *assumed* geometric albedo p∈[.04,.25] and update D(km)=1329×10^(-H_V/5)/sqrt(p). This yields D≈1530 km (p=.04), 967 km (p=.10) and 612 km (p=.25). In a single linear km reference chart, compare with Pluto's ~2,377 km diameter. No color, shape, orbital position or uncertainty probability density is encoded. Static chart, science, measurements and source URLs remain visible without JavaScript. User slider/three presets update the chart, accessible explanation and selected numeric diameter; 44px touch targets; no autoplay or extra dependencies.
