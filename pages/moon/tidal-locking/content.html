The comparison above answers the two questions people most often confuse.

Does the Moon rotate?

Yes — once every orbit

From space, the Moon turns once on its axis during each orbit around Earth. If it did not rotate, we would see different lunar faces during the month.

Why one face?

Rotation period = orbital period

The matching periods keep nearly the same lunar hemisphere pointed toward Earth. Small librations let us see about 59% over time.

Is this unique?

No — synchronous moons are common

Many large moons in the solar system are tidally locked to their planets. The Earth–Moon system is a familiar example, not an exception.

What's simplified

Why did this happen in the first place?

01

It wasn't always this way

Earth's gravity gradually slowed the Moon's rotation over hundreds of millions of years (tidal friction) until it locked into this 1:1 match.

02

Not unique to the Moon

Tidal locking happens throughout the solar system — many moons of Jupiter and Saturn are locked to their planets the same way.
