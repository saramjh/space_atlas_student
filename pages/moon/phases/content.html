Moon phase simulator: why the Moon changes shape

The Moon does not change shape. Half is always lit by the Sun; what changes is how much of that lit half we can see from Earth. Drag the orbit slider or jump to a major phase and compare both views.

Try the simulator

Three experiments that explain Moon phases.

Change one thing at a time and use the Earth view to test your prediction.

01

New → full Moon

Jump to New Moon, then Full Moon. The Moon itself stays half sunlit; only the viewing geometry changes.

02

Quarter does not mean 25% lit

At first and last quarter, roughly half of the visible disk is illuminated. "Quarter" describes the Moon's position in the cycle.

03

Separate phases from eclipses

Ordinary phases happen every month. Earth's shadow only causes a lunar eclipse when the Sun, Earth, and Moon line up closely enough.

What's simplified

This model compresses a few things to stay readable.

Every visualization on Space Atlas states what has been simplified, so a model is never mistaken for the evidence itself.

01

Distance

The real Earth–Moon distance is about 30 Earth diameters. Here it's compressed so the Moon stays visible next to Earth.

02

Tilt

The Moon's orbit is tilted about 5° from Earth's orbit around the Sun. This model flattens that tilt, which is also why real eclipses are rarer than this diagram would suggest.

03

Size

Earth and the Moon are enlarged relative to the orbit so both stay clearly visible and clickable-scale.
