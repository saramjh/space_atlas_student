Why are there two high tides a day, not one?

If tides were just the Moon "pulling" water toward it, there would be one high tide a day, on the side facing the Moon. In a simple ocean-covered-Earth model there are two — because tides come from differences in gravitational attraction, not simple pulling.

What's simplified

Why two bulges, not one?

01

Near side: pulled harder

Ocean water on the side facing the Moon is pulled more strongly than Earth's center, stretching a bulge toward the Moon.

02

Far side: left behind

Water on the far side is pulled less than Earth's center is, so it's effectively "left behind" — stretching a second bulge away from the Moon.

03

Earth rotates through both

As Earth spins once a day, most coastlines pass through both bulges — many places experience two high tides in a lunar day of about 24 hours 50 minutes. Actual local patterns also depend on geography and ocean dynamics.
