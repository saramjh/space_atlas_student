Sources · Question

Which NASA site should I actually open?

NASA, JPL, and ESA all publish space-science material for students, but they serve different jobs. This page is not a ranking — it's a guide to which source matches the question you actually have.

Quick picker

Start from what you need, not the site's name.

If you want

A short, simple explanation

Open NASA Space Place ↗ — built specifically for elementary and early-middle readers, with short articles, games, and basic vocabulary.

If you want

A hands-on project to build or test

Open JPL Education ↗ — grade-linked STEM activities that turn mission science into something you can do and measure.

If you want

To explore in 3D yourself

Open NASA's Eyes ↗ — a richer browser-based 3D simulation with real mission data, moons, asteroids, and time controls.

If you want

Precise facts and deeper science

Open NASA Science ↗ — planet facts, stars, galaxies, black holes, missions, and images for middle school through adult readers.

If you want

A second agency's perspective

Open ESA ↗ — European missions, science stories, and education material, useful for comparing how different agencies explain the same discovery.
