What is a black hole, actually?

A black hole is not a cosmic vacuum cleaner sucking in everything nearby — it's a region where gravity is so strong that not even light can escape. Here's what each part of the classic diagram actually means.

Visual model

A labeled diagram, not a photo.

  1. 01
    SingularityThe theoretical point at the center where matter is compressed to infinite density — physics as we know it breaks down here.
  2. 02
    Event horizonThe boundary past which nothing, not even light, can escape. This is the "black" in black hole — not a surface, just a point of no return.
  3. 03
    Photon ringLight from behind the black hole can bend around it, creating a bright ring — this is what the famous 2019 Event Horizon Telescope image actually captured.
  4. 04
    Accretion diskGas and dust spiraling in, heated to extreme temperatures — this glowing disk is often the only reason a black hole is visible at all.
What's simplified

This is a schematic

Real images (like the Event Horizon Telescope's) are blurry, radio-wavelength composites — this diagram exaggerates and labels features for clarity, the same "illustration, not photograph" distinction covered elsewhere on this site.

02

Not every black hole has a visible disk

A black hole with nothing nearby to pull in has no accretion disk and emits no light at all — it's detected only through its gravity's effect on nearby stars.
