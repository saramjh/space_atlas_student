Why do some stars end quietly and others explode?

Stars form as gas clouds collapse under gravity. Their initial mass strongly influences how quickly they consume fuel and whether they end as white dwarfs, neutron stars or black holes — though the details also depend on how they evolve.

Key process: Gravity overcomes local support and begins collapse.

Pathways are simplified. Some massive stars evolve differently because of winds, composition or binary interactions; their final remnants are not determined by one precise mass cutoff. In the illustrative tracks here, Sun-like stars form a planetary nebula and leave a white dwarf, while massive stars may undergo core collapse and leave a neutron star or black hole. This model is not a time-lapse or size-to-scale animation.
