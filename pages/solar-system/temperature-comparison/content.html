Why is Venus hotter than Mercury?

Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun. Venus is the second closest — and yet Venus is the hottest planet in the solar system, hotter than Mercury by over 250°C. Distance from the Sun isn't the whole story.

What kind of temperature is each figure?

The NASA comparison is not a set of eight comparable solid-surface measurements. Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars use averages over rocky surfaces; Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune have no solid landing surface, so their values refer to approximately Earth's sea-level pressure (1 bar) in the atmosphere. Mercury's mean hides extreme day–night variation.

What's simplified

Distance isn't the deciding factor. Atmosphere is.

01

Venus's thick CO₂ blanket

Venus's atmosphere is about 96% carbon dioxide and 90× thicker than Earth's, trapping heat in a runaway greenhouse effect.

02

Mercury has almost none

Mercury has essentially no atmosphere to trap heat, so its dayside bakes while its nightside can drop to -180°C — the "average" hides an enormous swing.

03

These are averages

A single number can't show day/night or pole/equator swings, which are extreme on airless worlds like Mercury and the Moon.
