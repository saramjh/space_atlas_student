Quick answers

Planet weight calculator: the useful numbers first.

These are illustrative Earth-scale-equivalent readings based on NASA effective equatorial gravity ratios (Earth = 1.00). The calculator updates them for the kg or lb value entered; it does not model standing on gas giants.

Mars

About 38% of Earth weight

A 150 lb Earth reading becomes about 57 lb on Mars. A 70 kg Earth reading becomes about 26.6 kg on a Mars-calibrated scale.

Jupiter

About 2.36× Earth weight

NASA’s effective equatorial gravity at Jupiter’s atmospheric 1-bar level is about 2.36× Earth’s, not a solid ground surface a person could stand on.

Formula

Earth reading × gravity ratio

For a familiar scale reading, multiply your Earth value by the planet's gravity relative to Earth. Mass itself does not change when you move between planets.

What's simplified

Why isn't this just "bigger planet, more gravity"?

01

Density matters too

Saturn is bigger than Earth but less dense than water — its effective equatorial gravity (~0.92×) is slightly less than Earth's, while much smaller Mercury and much larger Neptune land close together too.

02

"Surface" is fuzzy on gas giants

Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune have no solid surface — the calculator uses NASA’s approximate equatorial effective gravity at a reference atmospheric level, not a solid surface. Gravity depends on latitude, rotation and altitude.
