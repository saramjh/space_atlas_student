Why isn't Pluto a planet anymore?

Pluto didn't move, shrink, or change in any way in 2006 — the definition of "planet" changed around it. Here's the exact three-part rule, and why Pluto fails just one of the three.

"Cleared its neighborhood" means a planet's gravity has swept its orbital path clear of other large debris. Pluto orbits within the Kuiper Belt, a region still full of similarly sized icy bodies — it hasn't (and can't) clear its lane.

What's simplified

It's not really about size.

01

Pluto isn't small for its neighborhood

Pluto is still the largest known dwarf planet by some measures. The issue is what surrounds it (thousands of Kuiper Belt objects), not Pluto's own size.

02

The rule is genuinely debated

Some planetary scientists argue "clearing the neighborhood" is a poor criterion since even Earth and Jupiter share their orbits with some asteroids. This page states the current official (IAU) definition, not the only possible one.
