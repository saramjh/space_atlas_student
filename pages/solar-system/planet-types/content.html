What's actually inside a planet?

The eight planets aren't variations on one design — they're built from three genuinely different structures. Here's what's really underneath the surface of each type.

Rocky planet Earth example

Mercury · Venus · Earth · Mars

Crust: a solid surface

Earth's thin rocky crust covers a rock-rich mantle. Unlike the giant planets, Earth has a solid surface.

Evidence: Earth's layers are constrained by seismic observations.

Gas giant Jupiter example

Jupiter · Saturn

Clouds, not ground

Jupiter's cloud tops are not a solid surface; hydrogen and helium grow denser with depth.

Evidence: spacecraft observations of clouds and gravity; deeper layers are inferred.

Ice giant Neptune example

Uranus · Neptune

An atmosphere with no ground

Neptune has a hydrogen-, helium- and methane-rich atmosphere; it gradually blends into denser fluid regions.

Evidence: atmospheric measurements; the deep interior is inferred from models.

What the interior comparison means

Rocky planets have solid crusts above rocky mantles and metal-rich cores. Jupiter and Saturn have no solid landing surface, and hydrogen changes state under immense pressure. Uranus and Neptune contain hot, dense water-, ammonia- and methane-bearing fluids — “ice” names their ingredients, not their temperature or state.

The rings do not indicate comparable depths: Earth's interiors are investigated with seismic waves, whereas giant-planet interiors are reconstructed from spacecraft gravity, magnetic-field and other measurements. Jupiter's central heavy-element-rich region may be diffuse rather than a small solid ball.

What's simplified

"Ice" doesn't mean what you think.

01

Hot "ice"

The "ices" in ice giants are compressed under extreme pressure and heat — they behave as dense fluids, not the frozen ice you'd recognize.

02

Models versus measurements

Earth has real compositional boundaries studied with seismic waves; the interiors of gas and ice giants are much less certain. Colors and circles here are schematic, not measured depths or rigid shells.
