What's actually inside a planet?

The eight planets aren't variations on one design — they're built from three genuinely different structures. Here's what's really underneath the surface of each type.

Visual model

Three cross-sections.

Terrestrial (rocky)

Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars

  • Solid rocky crust and mantle
  • Dense metal (mostly iron) core
  • Small, dense, few or no moons

Gas giant

Jupiter, Saturn

  • Deep hydrogen and helium atmosphere
  • Layer of liquid metallic hydrogen
  • Small rocky/icy core, no solid surface

Ice giant

Uranus, Neptune

  • Thinner hydrogen/helium atmosphere
  • Deep mantle of water, ammonia, and methane "ices"
  • Small rocky core
What's simplified

"Ice" doesn't mean what you think.

01

Hot "ice"

The "ices" in ice giants are compressed under extreme pressure and heat — they behave as dense fluids, not the frozen ice you'd recognize.

02

No sharp boundaries

Real planetary interiors transition gradually between layers — these diagrams draw clean rings for clarity, not because there's a hard edge.
