Is visible light all there is to see in space?

Your eyes detect only a tiny sliver of the electromagnetic spectrum. Most of what space telescopes actually observe — radio waves, infrared, X-rays — is completely invisible to us. Drag across the bar to explore each band.

01

No hard edges

Wavelength changes smoothly — "infrared" fades into "visible" fades into "ultraviolet" with no sharp line, these are just useful labels.

02

Most bands need space telescopes

Earth's atmosphere blocks most X-rays, gamma rays, and much infrared — a major reason telescopes like Hubble, Chandra, and JWST are in space.
