Is visible light all there is to see in space?

Your eyes detect only a tiny sliver of the electromagnetic spectrum. Astronomers study radio, infrared, X-ray, and other light invisible to us. Select a band or explore the logarithmic wavelength scale to see what changes — and why.

Approximate wavelength ranges

NASA's reference ranges are approximate; band boundaries are conventions, not physical walls. Radio extends beyond the left edge and gamma rays beyond the right.

What's simplified

The spectrum is continuous, not seven boxes.

01

No hard edges

Wavelength changes smoothly — "infrared" fades into "visible" fades into "ultraviolet" with no sharp line, these are just useful labels.

02

Most bands need space telescopes

Earth's atmosphere blocks most X-rays, gamma rays, and much infrared — a major reason telescopes like Hubble, Chandra, and JWST are in space.
