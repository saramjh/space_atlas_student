02 · Learning method

Three steps from “that looks cool” to “I understand why.”

Visuals should start a question, not end it. Each topic follows the same learning pattern so students can separate a model from the evidence behind it.

STEP 1

Manipulate the model

Rotate a planet, change your viewpoint, or compare worlds side by side. Use motion to reveal relationships that a flat illustration hides.

STEP 2

Compare a measurable fact

Switch from a dramatic view to a scale view. Read diameter, distance, orbital period, or temperature as quantities that can be checked.

STEP 3

Verify the source

Follow the reference to NASA, JPL, ESA, or a mission page. Learn where the number, image, or simulation came from and what it can tell you.

04 · Read visualizations carefully

A useful space image tells you what kind of truth it shows.

A photograph, an artist's concept, a simulation, and a scientific diagram can all be useful, but they do not make the same claim.

OBSERVATION

Recorded by an instrument

Ask which telescope, spacecraft, wavelength, date, and processing steps produced the image or measurement.

MODEL / SIMULATION

A calculation made visible

Ask which variables were simplified, whether time or distance is compressed, and which data constrain the model.

ILLUSTRATION

An explanation, not a photograph

Useful for things we cannot photograph directly. A trustworthy source should label an artist's concept or schematic clearly.
